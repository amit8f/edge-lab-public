# V2: re-judging every finished screen against factors and luck

Registered 2026-10-15, before any factor regression or multiple-testing adjustment of these results was
computed. Requested by Amit. V2 re-scores existing results; it adds no trading idea and does not add to
the idea count (phase 2: 13; phase 1 + phase 2: 28).

## Question

1. Once the standard factors are accounted for, does any finished idea earn a return the factors don't
   already explain?
2. Once the number of ideas tried is accounted for, is any result distinguishable from luck?

## Inputs

- Every `research/results/<ID>/<ID>_primary_series.csv` that exists when V2 runs. Today that is M4,
  M5, M6, M7 (monthly signals, 126-trading-day holds) and M10, P3 (weekly signals, 21-trading-day
  holds). M11, M1 and M3 are included if their screens have finished by then. The series are used
  exactly as written, after each idea's own costs.
- Kenneth French's daily Fama-French 5 factors (2 x 3) and daily momentum, downloaded at run time,
  sha256 saved.

## Method

**Window factor returns.** For each signal date t, the holding window runs from the next trading day
(entry at its open) to the idea's exit day (h trading days after t: 126 or 21), on French's daily
calendar. Each factor's window return is the sum of its daily returns over the window, entry day
included (the open-to-close part of the entry day is approximated by the full day, disclosed), and so
is RF's.

**Factor-adjusted excess (primary).** Regress each idea's per-date excess series (picks minus universe
minus costs) on the six window factor returns (Mkt-RF, SMB, HML, RMW, CMA, Mom). The intercept is the
factor-adjusted excess per window. Standard errors: Newey-West with the idea's own lags (5 for the
monthly ideas, 6 for the weekly ones). Also reported: the picks' own alpha (picks minus RF on the same
factors), every factor loading, and R².

**Multiple testing.**

- **Romano-Wolf stepdown** on the factor-adjusted series. Weekly ideas are averaged to calendar months
  (mean of that month's per-date intercept-plus-residual values), and all ideas are aligned on their
  common months. Studentized moving-block bootstrap: blocks of 6 months, 10,000 joint draws, seed
  20261015, one-sided (H0: factor-adjusted excess <= 0). Reported: adjusted p per idea.
- **Holm** adjustment of the per-idea Newey-West p-values, as a simpler cross-check.
- **Deflated Sharpe ratio** (Bailey and López de Prado, 2014) for each idea. The inputs are:
  - the annualised information ratio from non-overlapping subsamples (every 6th month for the monthly
    ideas, every 4th week for the weekly ones), averaged across the offsets;
  - the series' skew and kurtosis;
  - N = 28 trials (phase 1 + phase 2);
  - the variance of the trial Sharpe ratios, estimated from the phase-2 ideas in the inputs.

  Reported: the probability that the true ratio is above zero after deflation.

**Placebo.** The same factor regression with factor windows shifted 52 weeks later (a misaligned
calendar). The loadings should collapse toward zero; if they don't, the window code is wrong.

## Verdict rule

An idea **survives** only if both hold:

- its factor-adjusted excess is above zero and clears the idea's own bar (Newey-West t >= 2.0 for M4 to
  M7 and M8-style ideas, t >= 3.0 for M10, M11 and P3); and
- its Romano-Wolf adjusted p is below 0.05.

No idea passed its raw screen, so the expected answer is that none survives. V2 still matters for two
reasons:

- It shows what each idea really was, through its factor loadings (for example, whether M10 was a
  high-volatility or small-cap bet).
- It sets the standard every future screen is judged by.

## Outputs

`research/results/V2/`: the window factor table, per-idea regressions (JSON and CSV), the
multiple-testing table, the placebo, and a REPORT.md.
