# P1: short penny stocks after dilution and distress events (penny track)

Registered 2026-10-15, before any trade of this idea. From plan v2. Phase-2 count at registration: 8
(main 6, news 1, penny 1). Phase 1 + phase 2 = 23.

## Question

Is shorting $1-5 stocks after offerings, delisting notices, reverse splits and late filings
profitable on paper, after realistic costs, borrow, stops and the easy-to-borrow limit?

Phase 1's descriptive study (in-sample 2019-2025) found these events predict declines; phase 1's I-12
and I-14 forward tests measure the decline itself. P1 asks the trading question. It is forward-only,
because the evidence is in-sample.

## Universe

Penny: Alpaca common stock (`core.instruments.classify`), raw close $1-$5 on the event day, median
20-day raw dollar volume >= $500k, at least 60 bars in the current series.

## Events (phase 1's frozen rules, unchanged)

From breakout-scanner commit 111b71d, `catalyst/rules.py` with `pipeline/p27c_classify.py` and
`catalyst/nt.py`, copied without changes:

- A8 offering: 8-K item 3.02, an offering form (S-1, S-1/A, S-3, 424B1-424B5), or an offering headline.
- A6 delisting notice: 8-K item 3.01.
- A9 reverse split: the bar rule E2 (price ratio jump >= 1.5 with matching volume ratio).
- NT: an NT 10-K or NT 10-Q filing.

Events cluster per stock and class within 2 days (the first counts). One open P1 position per stock.

## Trade

Signal: the nightly job (after 10pm ET) that first sees the event. Tradeable only if the stock is
shortable and easy to borrow on Alpaca's asset list of that night; others are logged as "not
tradeable" and counted. Entry: short at the next open. Exits (paper book, penny settings): stop at 3 x
ATR(20) above the fill, capped at +35%, checked on the close and filled at the next open; time exit at
the close of the 20th trading day. Costs: 2% round trip and 20% a year borrow (`config/risk.yaml`).
Sizing: 0.25% of the account at risk to the stop, at most 5 positions.

## Control

For each trade, a stock drawn at random (numpy default_rng(20261015 + date ordinal)) from the same
night's penny universe that is shortable and easy to borrow and had no P1 event in the prior 20
trading days, shorted with the same rules.

## Primary test

Per closed trade: net return of the trade minus net return of its control. Analysis at 100 closed
trades, or on 2027-10-29 if fewer: the mean, a t-statistic clustered by entry week, and a 95% CI.
**Pass** only if there are at least 100 trades, the mean > 0, t >= 2.0 and the CI's lower bound > 0.
Fewer than 100 trades at the ceiling date: UNDERPOWERED, reported, never a pass.

## Reported alongside (not tests)

Paper P&L and drawdown of the P1 sleeve; per event class; share of events not tradeable; stops hit;
the worst 10 trades.

## Stop rule

If the P1 sleeve falls 10% from its peak, P1 pauses for review (`config/risk.yaml`). A failed or
underpowered test ends P1.

## Clarifications (2026-10-15, before any trade; the job does nothing before 2026-11-02)

The nightly job is `tracks/penny/nightly.py`, run by `.github/workflows/leads.yml`.

1. Penny A8 is an 8-K with item 3.02 or an offering form. The frozen rules apply the offering *headline*
   (A8 text) to MAIN stocks only, so P1 has no headline A8. The "or an offering headline" above is
   void; the frozen code wins, as the Events section says.
2. NT events are timed like the frozen filing events: the first article tagging the stock within
   [acceptance - 24 h, acceptance + 2 h], else the EDGAR acceptance time. They cluster like the others.
3. Day d is the articles up to 23:30 ET of d (from the previous night's cutoff), SEC's daily index of d
   (filings dated d) and d's bars. The job runs after 23:30 ET and processes every trading day it hasn't
   processed yet, in order; a day whose index is not published yet waits for the next run.
4. A day processed late (a dropped or delayed run) still trades in the paper book, from the next open
   after d, because it uses only what was public by d 23:30 ET. The shortable and easy-to-borrow flags
   are those of the run's asset list.
5. The first signal day is 2026-11-02 (entries at the 2026-11-03 open). The 30 calendar days before it
   are processed for events only (warm-up), so clusters and the control's exclusion window start full.
6. Two P1 events for one stock on one night give one signal (the earliest by event time); the other is
   logged as "same stock, same night".
7. Stop distances use ATR(20) / close from adjusted bars (`adjustment=all`), so a split inside the 20
   days does not distort them, times the raw close. Fills, prices and the universe use raw bars.
8. The series of the universe rule (>= 60 bars, no gap over 15 calendar days) is computed per symbol;
   the cluster and control windows key on the symbol.
9. Controls: one numpy `default_rng(20261015 + date ordinal)` per night, one draw per P1 signal the
   book accepted, without replacement within the night, from the sorted pool. The 20 trading days of
   the exclusion window include the signal day. Names the control book already holds are left out.
   The control book has the same trade rules with no position, drawdown or account limits.
10. Each paper trade is also sent to Alpaca's paper account the same night when the run comes before
    the next open: market-on-open orders for entries and stop exits, market-on-close for time exits.
    Fills are read back the next night and logged with their implementation shortfall. The paper book,
    not these orders, is the trade record for the primary test.
11. Each P1 signal also records Interactive Brokers' published borrow fee and shares available for the
    stock that night (`core/borrow.py`). The book keeps charging 20% a year; P1's analysis also reports
    the net result with the logged fee (descriptive), and the share of signals whose fee is above 20%.

## Amendment (2026-10-15, before any trade): early stopping

Amit approved a sequential version of the primary test, so P1 can end early when the answer is clear.
The test statistic (mean, t clustered by entry week) is computed at 50, 75 and 100 closed trades, at
whichever of these counts is reached before the ceiling date:
- **Early pass:** at 50 trades if t >= 2.78, at 75 if t >= 2.30, at 100 if t >= 2.04 (mean > 0 in each
  case). These are the Lan-DeMets O'Brien-Fleming boundaries for a 5% two-sided (2.5% one-sided) test at
  information fractions 0.5, 0.75 and 1, computed by simulation. The 100-trade bar replaces the "t >= 2.0
  and CI above 0" above.
- **Early stop for futility (non-binding):** at 50 or 75 trades, if t <= 0, P1 stops and is reported as
  FAILED.
- Otherwise P1 continues. The minimum of 100 trades for a final pass and the 2027-10-29 ceiling stay;
  an early pass at 50 or 75 trades is a pass.
