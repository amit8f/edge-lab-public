# X1: limit orders vs market-on-open for paper entries (execution comparison, not an idea)

Registered 2026-10-15, before any paper trade. Like C0, X1 tests no edge and is not counted. It answers
whether P1/P2 entries should use limit orders instead of market-on-open orders.

## Design

The nightly job (`tracks/penny/nightly.py`) runs a third book, the limit book, on exactly the same
signals as the P1/P2 paper book, with the same rules except the entry:
- Each entry is a day limit order placed before the open: buys at the signal close x 1.01, short sells
  at the signal close x 0.99.
- Fill from the entry day's raw bar (`paper/book.py` limit_fill): at the open when the open is already
  through the limit; at the limit when the day's low (buys) or high (sells) goes strictly through it;
  otherwise no trade.
- A filled limit entry pays no entry cost (it crosses no spread); exits stay market orders and pay half
  the round trip, as in the paper book.

## Measures (reported with P1/P2's analysis, never a pass or fail)

- Fill rate, overall and per idea.
- For trades filled in both books: the entry price difference.
- The mean net return per signal in each book, counting unfilled limit orders as 0, and its
  difference with a CI clustered by entry week.

Daily bars can't show the order of the low and the limit inside the day, and a touch at the limit may
not fill in the queue, so the limit book is optimistic for fills at the limit. Alpaca's paper fills
(the mirror) don't settle this either; only small real orders would.

## Decision rule

If, at P1/P2's analysis, the limit book's mean net return per signal is higher with a CI above zero,
later penny ideas use limit entries. P1/P2's own test always uses the paper book.
