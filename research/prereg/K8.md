# K8: the AI wide bots, acted on once a day (main track)

Registered 2026-10-28, before any slow book exists. Source: Amit's "add all of those" (2026-10-28) after the
sweep found both wide bots losing far more than their coin twins while trading a lot (by Oct 28, C had made
92 trades and was down 10.3%, C-groq 237 trades and down 15.6%; their twins were down 2.3% and 4.5%). A
Bloomberg piece the same week on public AI trading contests found the same pattern: models in profit in 6 of
32 results, and under the same instructions one model made 1,418 trades and another 158.
Phase-2 count at registration: 58 (main 28, penny 10, news 9, cross-track 10, crypto 1). Phase 1 + phase 2 = 73.

This change was picked after seeing the books' results, so its decisive test is forward data only
(PROTOCOL rule 23). K4 and K7 keep running unchanged.

No real money. Fills are simulated in our own ledger; the slow books have no Alpaca mirror.

## Mechanism (PROTOCOL rule 10)

1. **Counterparty.** No new one. K8 asks whether the AI books lose mainly through turnover: each trade pays
   0.1% a side and the spread to whoever makes the market, and an hourly change of mind mostly trades
   intraday noise. Individual investors who trade most earn the least (Barber & Odean 2000, "Trading Is
   Hazardous to Your Wealth").
2. **Why us.** Not applicable (a fix to our own bots).
3. **Published.** The overtrading result is old and public; the AI-contest pattern is from October 2026.

## Rules (fixed now)

- Two new $10,000 books, `C-slow` and `C-groq-slow` (`tracks/main/ai_wide.py`, `SLOW`). Each uses its parent's
  answers (C's Gemini answer, C-groq's Groq answer) from the same runs: no extra AI calls, same prompt,
  parser, sizing (up to 5 positions, a fifth of equity each), costs (0.1% a side, 3% a year borrow), prices and
  $500 stop rule.
- A slow book acts once per ET day, at its parent's first readable answer with a price time at or after
  15:30 ET, from 2026-10-29 on (or the first trading day this code runs, if later). It then holds overnight
  until the next day's 15:30 answer. An unreadable or failed answer doesn't use up the day. With no readable
  answer from 15:30 to the close, the book holds.
- If the parent book stops, its slow book gets no more answers and holds what it has.
- The slow books' holdings are priced every run but stay off the parents' shortlist, so C and C-groq see
  exactly what they saw before K8.
- Coin twins `C-slow-coin` and `C-groq-slow-coin` take each new slow-book position with a coin-flip side
  (K4's `coin_side`) and drop it when the slow book does.

## Primary test

For each pair (C-slow vs C, C-groq-slow vs C-groq): the daily net return of the slow book minus its parent,
each from equity at the day's last run. Newey-West t with 5 lags; moving-block bootstrap with 5-day blocks,
10,000 draws, seed 20261028. **Pass** for a pair: mean > 0, t >= 3 and the bootstrap 95% CI above zero, after
at least 120 trading days from the slow book's first trade. Before then, no conclusion; a 60-day look is
exploratory. Each pair is judged on its own; two pairs at t >= 3 leave little room for luck.

## Secondary (no pass rule)

- Slow book minus its coin twin, daily net, NW t (5 lags): does the slow AI pick better than chance?
- Trades, turnover and costs paid per book, beside the parents'.
- Factor alpha (PROTOCOL rule 11) on the monthly difference, reported once 6 months exist.

## What happens next (fixed now)

A pair that passes goes in the Sunday report, and the next monthly review may stop the parent book (Amit's
call); the slow book keeps running. A pair that fails changes nothing: the books keep running as they are and
any further change is a new registration.

## Prediction written now

About 60% that each slow book beats its parent on mean (fewer trades, less cost), about 15% that either
reaches t >= 3 within 120 trading days, and neither beats its coin twin.
