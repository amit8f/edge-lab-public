# W1: who knows first? Published leak reports, unexplained trading and unusual options (three parts)

Registered 2026-10-17, before any of this idea's data was downloaded or looked at. Amit's requests:
find buying and selling trends ourselves, find what isn't widely seen or understood, find leak reports,
including from smaller outlets that get little attention, and act before the crowd.

**Legal line (applies to every part).** We use only information published to everyone: a news article
anyone can read or buy, an SEC filing, a public trade print. We never seek, buy or trade on material
non-public information, and that includes a "leak" passed on privately, second-hand or before it is
published, whoever it came from (US law reaches people who trade on a tip they know was passed in
breach of duty, however many hands it went through). A report counts from its publication time. We
don't trade on any partner's research before it is published (some investigative outlets share
findings with a fund first; we only ever see the published article).

W1 has three parts, each with its own primary test, and they are counted as three ideas (W1a, W1b, W1c).
Phase-2 count at registration: 27 (main 16, penny 4, news 3, cross-track 3, crypto 1). Phase 1 + phase
2 = 42.

## Mechanism (PROTOCOL rule 10)

- **Counterparty.** (a) Traders who only notice a deal or short report once the big outlets or the
  Benzinga wire repeat it. (b) Sellers on days when someone better informed is quietly buying, before
  an announcement explains the volume. (c) Option sellers (market makers) who quote calls before news
  that someone else already expects.
- **Why us.** Funds watch Bloomberg and Reuters in milliseconds; nobody can beat them there. Smaller and
  foreign-language outlets (a UK rumour site, an Israeli business paper, a short seller's PDF) are read
  by fewer people, and for $50M-$2B companies the profit is too small for a big desk to chase. A $5k
  account can take a small edge that a fund can't scale.
- **Decay and prior evidence.** Ahern & Sosyura (2015, Review of Financial Studies, "Rumor Has It"):
  about one third of 501 press merger rumours led to a bid within a year; rumours from experienced,
  industry-specialist reporters came true more often, and target prices overreact on average to rumours
  that don't come true. Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin (2001): stocks with unusually high volume earn
  more over the next month. Augustin, Brenner & Subrahmanyam (2019): abnormal options volume before
  M&A announcements. All three effects are well known; expect them to be small or gone in large caps.
  A fail is the likely outcome.

## Dates and holdout

Every historical window ends before 2025-10-01 (exits included). The forward data from 2026-11 on is
the confirmatory test for anything that passes.

## W1a: published leak and rumour reports, by source (news track)

**Events.** Benzinga articles from Alpaca's news history, 2016-01-01 .. 2025-09-23, tagged with at most
2 symbols, whose headline both cites a source from the list below and matches a deal or short-report
word (takeover, buyout, bid, approach, acquire/acquisition, merger, "explore/exploring a sale",
"strategic alternatives", "go private", stake, activist, "in talks", "short report", "short seller",
fraud). Stocks: common stock, raw close >= $1 and median 20-day raw dollar volume >= $500k on the
prior trading day (L1's universe).

**Source groups (fixed now; matched in the headline, case-insensitive):**

| Group | Sources |
|---|---|
| Big | Bloomberg, Reuters, Wall Street Journal/WSJ, Financial Times/FT, CNBC, New York Times, Dow Jones |
| Small (English) | Betaville, Dealreporter, The Information, Semafor, Axios, Business Insider, New York Post, Sky News, The Times, Sunday Times, Fox Business, Puck, The Deal, Capitol Forum, Debtwire, Octus, Reorg, StreetInsider, The Fly, Hunterbrook, Breakingviews, Barron's, MarketWatch |
| Foreign-language press | Calcalist, Globes, TheMarker, Nikkei, Handelsblatt, Il Sole 24 Ore, Les Echos, Expansion, Korea Economic Daily, Yonhap, Economic Times, Caixin, Jiji |
| Unnamed | "people familiar", "sources say", "according to sources", "chatter", "rumor/rumour", "report says" with no listed source |
| Short seller | Muddy Waters, Spruce Point, Culper, Kerrisdale, Fuzzy Panda, Grizzly, Wolfpack, Iceberg, Blue Orca, J Capital, Gotham City, Hindenburg, Morpheus, Bleecker Street, Viceroy, Citron, Scorpion, Hunterbrook (when the headline says short) |

An article matching two groups goes to the first in this order: short seller, small, foreign, big,
unnamed. The first article per symbol and source group in 5 trading days is the event; later repeats
are dropped.

**Direction.** + for deal or stake words, - for short-seller and fraud words.

**Entry and exit.** Entry at the first regular-session 1-minute SIP bar (`adjustment=all`) starting at
least 30 minutes after the article's created time (overnight articles: the 10:00 bar of the next
session). Exit at the close of trading day +5. Return = signed (stock - SPY over the same minutes),
minus C0's measured round trip for the stock's bucket (0.2% main, 2% penny before C0 exists) and borrow
on short trades (3% / 20% a year). The 30-minute wait is the point: it asks whether anything is left
for someone who reads the report late.

**Primary test (per source group; the 5 groups are counted together as W1a).** A group passes only if
it has >= 100 events, the mean tradable return > 0 with a date-clustered Newey-West t >= 3.0 (date-level
means, 2 lags), the 95% moving-block bootstrap CI (blocks of 5, seed 20261017) above 0, and the
Fama-French 5 + momentum alpha of its monthly returns > 0 with t >= 2.0. A significant move the other
way (t <= -3) is not a pass; it is written down as a hypothesis for a new registration.

**Control.** The same entry and exit minutes on a stock drawn from the same day's universe and price
group (default_rng(20261017 + date ordinal)), same sign.

**Coverage first ("does this source reach us at all?").** Before any return is computed, the code
counts events per source and year. A source with fewer than 20 events in 10 years is reported as "not
in our feed". Those sources are candidates for direct collection from their own free RSS feeds (see
the forward part below); nothing about them is concluded from Benzinga.

**Data QA.** 50 random events are checked by hand (Claude, logged in `W1a_check.csv` with ok y/n and a
note): right company, right source group, really a deal or short report. Fewer than 40 right stops W1a;
the matching is fixed under a new registration.

**Exploratory, decided now.** Confirmed vs unconfirmed rumours (a deal or merger 8-K/DEFM14A/SC TO-T for
the symbol within 12 months), 1-day and 20-day exits, the move in the 30 minutes we skip (how much the
first readers took), penny vs main, and per-source hit rates for the small and foreign groups.

**Forward part (measurement only, no trades).** If Amit approves the extra Actions minutes, the private
news collector also stores the publication time and headline of free RSS feeds from small and foreign
outlets that turn out "not in our feed" or that lead Benzinga, and measures, as L1 does for filings,
how long Benzinga takes to repeat them. No feed is added without his yes.

## W1b: unexplained heavy trading (cross-track: main and penny)

**Signal (daily, computable on the day).** For each stock in L1's universe on day t (2016-03-01 ..
2025-09-15): volume z = (log volume_t - mean of log volume over t-60..t-1) / its standard deviation, from
raw daily SIP bars. A day is **unexplained heavy buying** when z >= 3, the close is in the top 30% of
the day's range ((close - low) / (high - low) >= 0.7), the day's return is between 0% and +10%, and
there is no Benzinga article naming the stock and no EDGAR filing by the company from t-3 to t (filing
date <= t, Forms 3/4/5 included). **Unexplained heavy selling** mirrors it (close location <= 0.3,
return between -10% and 0%).

**Trade.** Long the buying names, short the selling names, from the next open to the close of day +10,
equal weight, main costs 0.2% / penny 2% round trip, borrow 3% / 20% a year on shorts. Price groups
are tested separately (main, penny) and both counted in W1b.

**Primary test.** Per price group, per direction: >= 200 events, mean tradable return > 0 with a
date-clustered Newey-West t >= 3.0 (5 lags), the block-bootstrap CI above 0, FF5 + momentum alpha t >= 2.0.

**Control.** Same-day random stocks from the same universe and price group with z < 1 and the same
close-location side.

**Footprints before announcements (descriptive, decided now).** For 400 random 8-K item 1.01 or 2.01
filings, 400 SC 13D originals and 400 merger-target announcements (DEFM14A / SC TO-T / SC 14D9 filings,
dated from the first 8-K naming the deal) in 2016-2025-09, and 400 same-day random controls: on trading
days -10 .. -1, the volume z, the share of volume printed off-exchange (Alpaca SIP trades with exchange
code D, i.e. FINRA trade reporting), the share of trades of 10,000+ shares, and the Lee-Ready buy-sell
imbalance from SIP trades and quotes. Reported with Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests vs controls. This part
explains; it trades nothing, because conditioning on a later announcement is look-ahead.

**Data QA.** 30 random signal days checked by hand against the bars and the news and filing lists.

## W1c: unusual call buying (main track)

**Data.** Alpaca historical option bars (OPRA), the only free options history we have, from
2024-02-01 .. 2025-09-15. The first stage checks that expired contracts are listed; if not, W1c is
logged "no data" and doesn't run.

**Signal.** For main-track stocks with listed options: on day t, call volume in contracts expiring
within 45 days and struck at least 5% above the prior close, divided by its own 20-day mean. A day is
**unusual** when that ratio >= 5, the day's call volume is >= 1,000 contracts and at least 3x put volume
in the same expiries, and there is no article or filing from t-3 to t (as in W1b).

**Trade.** Long the stock (not the options) from the next open to the close of day +10, 0.2% round
trip. One event per stock per 10 days.

**Primary test.** >= 150 events, mean > 0 with a date-clustered Newey-West t >= 3.0 (5 lags), CI above
0, FF5 + momentum alpha t >= 2.0. Control: same-day random option-listed stocks with ratio < 1.5.

**Exploratory.** Share of events followed within 30 days by a deal 8-K or SC 13D, vs control.

## Stop rules

A part that fails ends as a trading idea and stays in the log. A part that passes gets a separate
forward paper test registration before any paper order. None of W1 places orders.

## Amendments (2026-10-17, before any W1 data was downloaded)

Amit asked for the deeper version (`rethink/DEEPER_FLOWS_2026-10-17.md` in the project files). These
change W1 before any of its data exists. The primary tests above are unchanged unless a line says so.
The idea count stays 27.

**W1a**
1. Articles are fetched with their summary, author (byline) and URL into W1a's own cache. The source
   group is still matched in the headline only. The summary is used only for the exploratory
   first-publisher table.
2. Story clustering: articles naming the same symbol within 2 trading days that share a deal or short
   word form one story. Each story records its first article's source group and author. The primary
   events stay as registered (the first article per symbol and source group in 5 trading days).
3. Resolution, using EDGAR as the referee. A deal or stake rumour came true if, within 365 days after
   the article, the company files an 8-K with item 1.01 or 2.01, or is the subject of an SC TO-T,
   SC 14D9, DEFM14A, 425 or SC 13D. A short report came true if, within 365 days, the company files an
   8-K item 4.02 (non-reliance on past financials) or 4.01 (auditor change) or an NT 10-K. Hit rates are
   reported per source group and per author with at least 5 resolved rumours, as Beta posteriors with a
   Beta(1, 2) prior (mean 1/3, the Ahern & Sosyura rate), with Brier scores.
4. Secondary test, counted inside W1a: the gap between the source and the price. The price-implied deal
   probability is p = (entry price - prior close) / (0.30 x prior close), clipped to [0, 1], 0.30 being a
   typical takeover premium. The source's hit rate h uses only rumours resolved before the event's date
   (point in time) from the same author if it has >= 5 such rumours, else the same source group. Long
   when h - p > 0.15, short when h - p < -0.15, deal and stake rumours only, the same entry, exit, costs
   and pass rule as the primary test.
5. Coverage: as well as Benzinga counts, the code asks GDELT's free DOC API how many articles each small
   and foreign outlet's domain published per year with deal words. If GDELT can't reach back to 2016, it
   logs "not available" and nothing else changes.
6. Forward forecast log (no trades): once the history stage has run, the private news collector logs
   every new deal-rumour or short-report headline with its source group, author and point-in-time h, in
   the hash-chained ledger. EDGAR resolves each one after 365 days, and the Brier score is reported.

**W1b**
7. Detector check against the SEC's own cases, before any return is computed. Claude reads SEC
   litigation releases from 2016-2025-09 that charge trading ahead of an announcement, in date order,
   until 60 usable cases (a US-listed stock and a dated announcement) are logged in `W1b_sec_cases.csv`.
   Recall is the share of cases with at least one W1b buying or selling signal day in the 10 trading
   days before the announcement. The control rate is the same stock's rate on 10-day windows ending 250
   to 500 trading days earlier. The detector is **validated** if recall is at least twice the control
   rate with a one-sided Fisher exact p < 0.05. The return test runs either way, because the
   high-volume premium doesn't need insiders, but an unvalidated W1b result is labelled so and can't be
   called evidence of informed trading.
8. Proxy-dated windows, descriptive: Claude reads the "Background of the Merger" section of 100 random
   DEFM14A filings (2016-2025-09) and logs the first-contact and NDA dates. The footprints are compared
   across three windows: 60 trading days before first contact, first contact to announcement, and the
   announcement's 10 days.
9. Trade signing in the footprints uses the quote midpoint (above mid = buy, below = sell, at mid =
   unsigned). Lee-Ready's tick test is the fallback only when no quote is within 1 second. Retail prints
   are off-exchange (venue D) prints whose price has a sub-penny fraction that isn't 0 or 0.5 cents (the
   BJZZ rule). The rule's published error rates are quoted beside every retail number.
10. FINRA OTC Transparency weekly volume per named ATS and per named non-ATS firm (from 2016) is added
    to the footprints: the share of a stock's volume that went through wholesalers (a retail proxy) and
    through ATSs, in event weeks vs the stock's own prior 26 weeks.

**New raw-source loggers (no counted idea).** These log forward from November, each append-only: Form
144 filings from EDGAR's daily index, SEC litigation releases, FTC early-termination notices and FINRA's
weekly OTC volume (all in the public repo, government data), plus the RSS feeds of the small and
foreign outlets in W1a's list (in the private repo's news collector, since headlines aren't
republished). A source that shows a repeatable lead over the first headline gets its own registration.

## Clarifications (2026-10-17, before any W1 data was downloaded)

The code is `tracks/news/w1a_leaks.py`, `tracks/main/w1b_volume.py` and `tracks/main/w1c_calls.py`, with
shared data in `tracks/news/w1_data.py` (workflow `w1-study`, manual runs only, its own data cache).

11. Factor alpha for W1a, W1b and W1c: the trades last 6 to 10 sessions, so the alpha regresses the
    per-entry-day mean return on the daily Fama-French 5 + momentum factors summed over each trade's
    days (N1's method), not on monthly returns.
12. W1a source matching: outlet names are matched case-sensitively. The names that are also ordinary
    words in title-case headlines (The Information, The Times, Puck, The Deal, The Fly, Globes, Nikkei,
    Expansion) count only with a citation cue ("according to", "per", "via", "citing", "reported by",
    a colon, or followed by "reports", "says" and the like). Generic short-seller names (Grizzly,
    Iceberg, Scorpion, Morpheus, Wolfpack, Gotham City, Bleecker Street) count only when followed by
    Research, Capital or Partners. A short-seller name counts only beside a short, fraud or report
    word; otherwise the next groups are tried. For the other groups the short words are "short
    report", "short seller", "short position" and "fraud".
13. W1a: an article tagged with two symbols gives one event per symbol with the same sign. The
    universe and price group come from the last session that closed before the article. The hand-check
    file holds the article URL and the matched words, not the headline (Alpaca's terms); Claude reads
    each article at its URL.
14. W1a resolution and hit rates use only events whose 365-day window ends by 2025-09-30, so no filing
    from the holdout period is read. The point-in-time rate h follows the same rule.
15. W1b and W1c returns are stock minus SPY (all-adjusted open of the entry day to the close of the exit
    day), as in W1a. The exit is the close of the 10th trading day counting the entry day as day 1.
16. W1b's SEC-case check: the Fisher test compares the cases with one random control window per case
    (default_rng(20261017)); the mean over all windows ending 250-500 trading days earlier is reported
    beside it.
17. W1b footprints: only stocks with a 20-day median dollar volume under $50M on the prior day (tick data
    for larger stocks is too big to fetch). A merger target's announcement date is its first 8-K item 1.01
    in the 180 days before the DEFM14A, SC TO-T or SC 14D9, else that filing's date. The 400 controls are
    random universe stock-days with the same size limit. A day with more than 400,000 trades or quotes is
    skipped and counted as "too dense".
18. W1c: "call volume" in the unusual rule means the short-dated out-of-the-money call volume. The main
    track is the universe with a raw prior close of at least $5. Strikes are compared with the raw prior
    close. Days without option volume count as 0 in the 20-day mean.
19. GDELT counts use English deal words, so non-English outlets will be undercounted; the counts are
    coverage only.
20. The SEC cases for amendment 7: a code stage (`w1b` `seclist`) reads every litigation release
    2016-01 .. 2025-09 and keeps those that charge insider trading and mention an announcement, an
    acquisition, a merger, a tender offer, a takeover or a buyout, as `W1b_sec_candidates.csv` (release,
    URL, the matching sentences). Claude reads the candidates in date order, opening each release, and
    logs the first 60 usable ones (a US-listed stock traded and a dated public announcement) in
    `W1b_sec_cases.csv` (release, symbol, announce_date, note).
