# V4: raw data instead of assumptions (costs, delistings, the headline classifier)

Registered 2026-10-15, before any number below was computed. Requested by Amit ("swap digested data
for raw data"). V4 re-measures inputs of finished results; it adds no trading idea and does not add to
the idea count (phase 2: 13; phase 1 + phase 2: 28).

## 1. Real trading costs from quotes

Every screen charged a flat round-trip cost: 0.2% for the main track and 2% for penny. Here each
historical pick is costed from Alpaca's consolidated (SIP) quotes instead:

- **Picks costed.** All M10 and P3 picks. For M4, M5, M6 and M7, 300 picks each, drawn with numpy
  default_rng(20261015).
- **Entry.** Median relative quoted spread, (ask - bid) / mid, from 09:31 to 09:40 New York time on
  the entry day, the first trading day after the signal date.
- **Exit.** The same median over 15:50 to 16:00 on the exit day, the 21st or 126th trading day after
  the signal date.
- **Valid quotes only.** Quotes count only when bid > 0, ask > bid and spread / mid < 50%.
- **Round-trip cost.** Half the entry spread plus half the exit spread. This is the cost of crossing
  the spread with market orders; impact for a $5,000 account is negligible next to it.
- **Missing quotes.** A pick with no valid entry quotes keeps the flat cost and is counted.

Reported per idea:

- the distribution of measured cost against the flat assumption;
- each primary series recomputed with measured costs (picks minus universe, the universe left
  uncharged as before);
- its mean and Newey-West t with the idea's own lags.

**Verdicts don't change in either direction from this.** A result that would pass with measured costs
is post-hoc. It becomes a hypothesis for a new pre-registered idea, never a pass.

## 2. Delistings inside a holding window

A pick with no quotes at all on its exit day (checked from the open to the close when the last 10
minutes had none) stopped trading before then: delisted, suspended or
acquired. Its return in the screens ran to its last close. The sensitivity re-costs these picks with
Shumway's (1997) average delisting returns for performance delistings: -30% for NYSE and NYSE American
listings, -55% for Nasdaq. They are applied to every such pick, because mergers can't be told apart
here. That makes this a worst-case bound, not an estimate. Reported per idea: the count of such picks
and the primary mean under the bound.

## 3. The analyst-headline classifier (M6, M8)

The sample comes from single-symbol Benzinga headlines, 2016-10 to 2025-09:

- **100 the classifier labelled as an action** (upgrade, raise, downgrade or cut), to measure
  precision;
- **100 it labelled as no action** but that contain at least one analyst word (upgrade, downgrade,
  price target, rating, initiates, maintains, reiterates, analyst), to measure what it misses.

Both are drawn with numpy default_rng(20261015).

The headlines are written to one file with only an id. The classifier's labels go to a separate file.
Claude labels every headline from the first file before the second is opened, with one of: up, down,
none. This is not independent of Claude. It is independent of the regex, and Amit is invited to
check any 30 of the labels himself.

Reported: precision of the up and down labels, the share of the no-action pool that is actually an
action, and every disagreement.

**Rule.** If precision of either direction is below 90%, M6 and M8 get a written caveat. A corrected
classifier becomes a new pre-registered idea.

## 4. Point-in-time SEC timestamps (no download; reasoning recorded now)

Every screen enters at the next open after the signal close. A filing EDGAR dates t was accepted by
22:00 New York time on t, so it was public before that next open. Ranking on "filed <= t" therefore
never trades on information that wasn't available at entry. Acceptance times to the second matter for
intraday event trades (N1), and N1 already uses Benzinga's own timestamps. So no existing result
depends on them.
